3. LANE-DEVIATION DIAGNOSIS

Findings supported by current evidence

- The generated path remains on the intended node-4 movement.
- Candidate 3 post-startup localization was stable: maximum position
  innovation was approximately `0.059 m`, with no localization event.
- Fixed curvature-only offsets did not prevent physical lane entry and
  increased outer-boundary risk.
- Candidate 3 frames show the real yellow divider moving across image center
  while map-reference absolute cross-track error over waypoints 200-400
  averaged approximately `0.027 m` and reached `0.074 m`.

Interpretation

Map-reference error is not a sufficient measure of physical lane clearance.
The evidence supports a mismatch among map reference, controller tracking
point/vehicle footprint, and physical boundary. It does not support claiming
that localization failure or Stanley gain alone is the root cause.

4. PROPOSED HYBRID NAVIGATION

Waypoint navigation retains global route and intersection responsibility. The
front CSI camera supplies local boundary evidence in stages:

1. measurement and warning — implemented;
2. confidence-gated, debounced slowdown/stop — proposed next;
3. small bounded steering correction — only after repeated validation.

YOLO and intersection stops retain authority. Camera logic must not select a
route or grant a conflict-zone movement.

6. REQUIRED BEFORE/AFTER EVALUATION

Keep route, start pose, speed, and baseline controller unchanged. Report:

- trial count, completed laps, and successful-lap rate;
- physical boundary violations;
- warning lead time and false warnings/stops;
- mean absolute, RMS, maximum, and 95th-percentile tracking error;
- straight-versus-curve results;
- steering saturation; and
- localization events.

Clearly distinguish map-reference error from camera physical-boundary evidence.
The camera measurement is image-space, not calibrated metric lane error.

7. CONTROLLED TWO-VEHICLE DEMONSTRATIONS

After individual lane reliability is acceptable, record:

1. simultaneous CZ1 approach: one vehicle proceeds, the other waits and then
   resumes after release; and
2. lead/follow behavior: the following vehicle responds when the leader stops.

Report waiting time, crossing success, occupancy time, communication recovery,
minimum separation, YOLO stops, and every collision or manual stop.
